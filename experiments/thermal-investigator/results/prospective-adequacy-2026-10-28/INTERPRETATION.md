# Interpretation

The prospective check adds one diagnostic experiment without refitting the model. Its result is saved before future challenge outputs are generated. All methods receive equal fitting data and one 120-second probe plus a 300-second reset charge.

Fixed sustained heating flagged 9/9 systems with at least one challenge error above 0.3°C; fixed release flagged 3/9, and supplied-model disagreement flagged 6/9. Sustained heating also flagged one of 21 systems whose three challenge errors stayed below that threshold. That flag represents measured mismatch on the probe itself, so it is not proof of an erroneous warning. These counts are system-level, not independent counts of every time sample.

The stronger result comes from a conventional fixed experiment. It establishes no Meta contribution and no superiority of intelligent experiment selection. Thirty new system instances were evaluated, but their three challenge schedules and hidden world families were already used in development. The result needs confirmation with new schedules and additional mechanisms before a reliability claim.

Next implementation target: retain a failed prospective check as explicit investigation state, revise a bounded model family, and demand another committed prediction before accepting the revision. Compare that loop with conventional numerical revision under the same total experiment budget. No new paid calls are justified by this result alone.
